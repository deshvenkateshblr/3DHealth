Let's Pause

Your health situation needs specialized care right now.

🤰 You're Expecting

Pregnancy transforms your entire health picture. Your labs, safety protocols, and screening needs are completely different—and specialized.

  • Different reference ranges: Hematocrit, glucose, hormones all shift during pregnancy
  • Contraindicated tests: Certain imaging and screenings must wait
  • Trimester-specific care: Each stage has its own protocol
  • Specialized screening: Gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, fetal development

Your prenatal team is the right guide

Your OB/GYN has the expertise to curate the perfect testing plan for your specific pregnancy. They know your individual risk factors and can time everything perfectly for you and your baby.

🏥 Recent Surgery or Procedure

Your body is in active recovery mode. Lab work and assessments done too soon can give misleading results.

  • Immune markers are elevated during healing
  • Inflammation markers (CRP, ESR) will be artificially high
  • Blood counts may be recovering
  • Recovery timeline varies: 6–12 weeks for most procedures; major surgery may need longer

Talk to your surgical team

Ask when it's safe to do general health screening. Most surgeons recommend waiting at least 6–8 weeks post-op for routine labs to settle. Your surgical team knows your specific procedure and can give you the green light.

🏨 Recent Hospital Stay

Your body has been through a significant medical event. Recovery labs should come from your care team, not a general assessment.

  • Your discharge summary includes a specific follow-up plan
  • Post-hospital labs are timed by your discharge team
  • Infection risk is still being monitored
  • Medication adjustments may still be happening

Follow your discharge plan

Your hospital gave you a post-discharge summary with specific lab timing and follow-up dates. Stick to that plan—it's custom-built for your recovery. Once you've cleared the recommended follow-up window (usually 2–6 weeks), you can come back here.

🩺 Under Active Medical Treatment

Right now, your care should stay coordinated with the doctors actively managing your condition.

  • Treatment timelines are specific: Labs during active care serve your treatment, not general assessment
  • Results can shift rapidly: What's true this week may not be true next week
  • Your doctors have context: They know your full medical story and goals
  • Overlap creates confusion: Multiple parallel assessments can muddy decision-making

Stay focused with your care team

Keep all your lab work and assessments flowing through your primary doctor or specialist. Once your active treatment phase ends and you've had a follow-up visit confirming stability, you can use this tool for a fresh baseline assessment.

💊 Recently Started New Medication

Your body is still adjusting. Lab values and how you feel are both in flux, which means results won't be reliable yet.

  • Adjustment period varies: 2–12 weeks depending on the medication
  • Side effects settle over time: What feels bad now may improve
  • Your prescriber has timing: They know when to re-test you
  • Baseline labs matter: Before-and-after comparison is key

Let your prescriber guide the timeline

Ask your doctor when it's safe to do general health screening—usually 6–8 weeks after starting a new medication. They may want to re-draw your "baseline" labs at that point, which will give you the clearest picture of how the med is working.

Timeline for coming back

Check back once you've cleared your care team's recommended follow-up window. This tool is perfect for baseline screening when you're stable and not in active recovery or treatment.
